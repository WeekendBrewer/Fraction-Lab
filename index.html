What makes the pieces a fraction?

The pieces must be equal in size. The denominator counts how many equal pieces make one whole, not how many shapes are on the screen. A denominator of 0 is undefined, so the explorer starts at 1.

To compare pictures fairly, use the same size whole. Half of a large cake can be more cake than half of a small cake, even though both are one half.

Why can’t I just compare the top numbers?

Piece size matters. One third is larger than one sixth: dividing the same whole into more pieces makes each piece smaller. With the same denominator, compare numerators. With different denominators, rename both fractions using equal-size pieces.

Why don’t we add the denominators?

The denominator names the size of each piece. One fourth plus two fourths is three fourths, not three eighths. If the sizes differ, first rename both fractions with a common denominator; then add the numerators.
